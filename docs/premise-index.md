# Premise index

**One line per card, naming the situation it is about, grouped so that cards
about the same thing sit next to each other.**

## Why this exists

Nine batches checked flags, speakers, gates, weights, chain structure and
balance. **Not one of them ever checked what a card was *about*.** Every rule in
[content-prompt.md](content-prompt.md) is satisfiable by a card whose premise
already exists three batches back, and the test suite will pass it, because
duplication of subject is invisible to every instrument this project has.

So the same good idea got reinvented every few batches. This index is the
missing instrument. It should have existed from batch 2.

**How to use it.** Before writing a card, find the family its situation belongs
to and read what is already there. If the premise is already written, either
write a different card or deliberately write the *better* version and retire the
older one. Do not add a third telling of anything.

**900 cards, 36 families.** Sorted by size, largest
first: a big family is not automatically a problem — `MOTHER` and `CHILDREN`
are large because the deck is about a family — but a big family is where
duplicates hide.

## Duplicate families — resolved

Five families where the same card had been written more than once. **Ten cards
were given genuinely new premises and two had a single duplicated clause cut.**
Every rewrite stayed *in slot*: same speaker, same category, same gate, same
weight, same effects, so no structure count and no balance figure moved.

The rule applied was **keep the best telling, not the first.** Batch order was
not a tiebreak — three of the ten rewritten cards are from batches 1 and 2, and
three are from batch 9.

### 1. An old man trades on your father's name — was 4

**Kept** `b4_008` (a story with *names* in it, told by a man who buried two
brothers for us — the decision is loyalty, not exposure) and `b4_019` (he wants
money on the strength of the story, which is a different ask).

**Rewritten:** `b1_021` → a man of ours carrying a pistol into Mass.
`b9_018` → one of the Ridgewood men has had business cards printed.

### 2. A small bill paid the wrong way for N months — was 6

The worst family in the deck. **Kept** `b3_033` (twenty-eight thousand dollars
to an address nobody can account for), `b3_032` (it implicates the uncle) and
`b3_005` (rewritten during the Aurelio pass; it is now about cash becoming
impossible rather than a stray line item).

**Rewritten:** `b5_029` → nine men on the books and nineteen on the floor.
`b7_038` → a crew ordering goods on the company's account. `b9_029` → three
crews using one office telephone, on one bill.

### 3. The nephew is casually adjacent to a policeman — was 7

**Defended as a motif, with one exception.** Matty's porousness to police is his
function in the deck — he is fearless because he has never seen a consequence —
and the six survivors are six different vectors: a venue (`b2_023`), a
girlfriend's father who is a *source* (`b3_022`), a girlfriend's brother who is
an *inspection* (`b4_020`), a gym acquaintance (`b3_018`), found property
(`b5_022`) and being seen by Ranieri (`b5_033`). Six ways of being porous is
characterisation. Two identical ways would not be.

**Rewritten:** `b1_025`, which was `b3_022`'s girl and `b3_022`'s father two
batches earlier → a card game in the club's back room that is not ours.

### 4. Somebody going round saying he is with us — was 9

**Kept** `b9_020` (the best telling — it ends on *do I have anybody?*),
`b6_076` (a chain opener), `b7_034` (selling the name as a service, which is a
different crime) and `b8_088` (Angelo's, and about him rather than the name).

**Rewritten:** `b7_019` → he asks to be the one who goes and collects.

### 5. A car outside, and the child who has stopped minding — was 11

**The one no instrument here could have caught**, and larger than it first
looked: six Giulia cards, five of which ended on a child greeting the watcher.

**Kept** `b8_065` (*she thinks they are friends of yours, and I do not know what
to tell her instead* — the best of them), `b1_010` (Anna crosses the road to
avoid it: fear, not habit) and `b9_012` (the school, and counting days).

**Clause cut** on `b6_073` and `b7_012`. Both were doing their own work and it
was one sentence that dragged them in. `b6_073` now ends on her sleeping through
the night, which is what that vice is actually about; `b7_012` now turns on the
schedule — *only Tuesdays, and never the same car*.

**Rewritten:** `b2_013` → a pinched man's wife and two boys in the spare room.
`b7_068` → Braca has posted two men outside the house, unasked.

**The non-Giulia members were left alone and are not duplicates.** `b1_082` and
`b9_023` are both *a car outside my mother's building*, but one is a man
accusing the player and the other is a boy proving he showed restraint;
`b4_037` is Ranieri's and `b8_063` is Matty spotting one of ours.

### 6. Giulia's Sunday table — a duplicate introduced during this work

`b9_013` was written in the batch-9 session as the fix for Giulia costing blood
on every card, and it duplicated `b1_015` (*Sunday I want the table to be us*).
**Kept `b1_015`; rewrote `b9_013`** → one of the men came to the door and asked
*her*, not the player, to hold a canvas bag for a week. It keeps the
`giulia_shielded` setter, which is exactly what the new ask means.
